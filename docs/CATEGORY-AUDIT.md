# Template category audit — 14 October 2026

All 25 topic tabs and the separate Community tab were inventoried from the actual catalog. We compared their purpose and format coverage with Canva's public template directory and Gaming galleries. This is a category audit, not a claim that every private, personalized or paid Canva template was inspected or reproduced.

## What changed

- 24 original Gaming Studio compositions: 20 native editable designs and four new illustrated designs with editable typography.
- 26 Everyday Stories compositions, adding a design to each of the 25 topics and a second Gardening design.
- Gaming browsing separates posters/event promos, banners/stream screens, and square social/team artwork. Search suggestions stay within the selected category.
- All topics now have their own heading and relevant search suggestions. Suggestions without matching catalog entries are omitted.
- The Reference Remix collection's 240 entries now appear as 58 default composition/photo/month groups. The 12 calendar months remain separate because their dates differ. Show all variations reveals every matching entry; a specific title/palette search still finds its variation.
- Changing topic clears incompatible collection, format and imagery filters. Saved bookmarks and existing template IDs remain valid.

The complete catalog contains **1447 entries**, including variants, 8 General designs and nine credited Community entries. Entries and layout families are different measures; neither is a count of unique photographs. Gaming now has 54 entries and 32 default designs, compared with 29 entries and seven non-repeated groups before the addition. The four new Gaming illustrations are recorded in the [asset manifest](../assets/gaming/PROVENANCE.json).

## Reference observations

Canva's [template directory](https://www.canva.com/templates/) organizes its library by useful deliverables such as posters, presentations, social posts, invitations and calendars. OpenCanvas previously organized many topics mainly by palette variants. That distinction motivated useful new compositions and the default grouping change.

The [Gaming poster gallery](https://www.canva.com/posters/templates/gaming/) and [Gaming banner gallery](https://www.canva.com/banners/templates/gaming/?continuation=100) demonstrate neon/controller imagery, tournaments, retro arcade, chess and channel branding. The [livestream gallery](https://www.canva.com/templates/s/livestream/?continuation=600) also includes waiting/intermission screens and episode announcements. These informed the new Gaming use cases and format sections, not copied source files or layouts.

The [podcast gallery](https://www.canva.com/podcasts/templates/), [garden gallery](https://www.canva.com/templates/s/garden/), [volunteer flyers](https://www.canva.com/flyers/templates/volunteer/) and [event logo gallery](https://www.canva.com/logos/templates/event/) illustrate why each category needs practical tools as well as promotional posters. New examples include a stream timetable, campaign brief, loyalty card, donation checklist, property-floor-plan illustration, city itinerary and plant-care log.

## Limits and verification

OpenCanvas remains much smaller in breadth, photographic variety and complete multi-page kits. Several small categories still contain only two or three designs. The new stream screens and tournament brackets are editable static graphics; they do not connect to broadcasts or calculate live scores. A single new template per topic improves coverage without establishing Canva feature parity.

Category, Gaming and discovery tests check unique IDs, geometry, search recovery, grouping, layer bounds, portable project round trips and native editing. All 50 new designs were rendered with the bundled fonts and reviewed in contact sheets; supplied text had no measured overflow. Existing community credits were preserved.
